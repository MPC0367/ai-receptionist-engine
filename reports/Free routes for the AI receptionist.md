# Test the Thai Receptionist Free, Pay Later

*First, the original question.* Reverse-engineering Kirx needed no money. It was done from the dossier you supplied and turned into O2's own clean-room blueprint. Under that same clean-room rule, this research did not look at Kirx at all. Everything below is about building and testing O2's own receptionist without spending.

O2 can get from today's simulation to a first real phone call answered by its own agent **without spending anything**, as long as it accepts two limits: the free models will not tell it how Claude behaves, and the first real call uses a free US number, not a Thai one. The planned ≈US$10 Claude comparison can be replaced at $0 by three things. The first is open models running on the M4 Pro through Ollama (Gemma 4, Qwen 3.6, Typhoon 2.5, gpt-oss). The second is Cerebras's free trial, which gives 1 million tokens a day each for gpt-oss-120b (the model behind O2's website adviser) and Qwen 3.8 27B. The third is Google's Gemini free tier, a frontier-class reference that must only ever see fictional data. Thai *hearing* is free and good: SCB 10X's open Typhoon speech models run on the Mac. Thai *speaking* is the weak link. Every Thai voice that runs locally is either limited to non-commercial use or has an unclear licence, so the best free voice is Microsoft Azure's free tier: half a million characters a month and three Thai neural voices. For calls, a free softphone app can dial the self-hosted LiveKit that already works on the Mac. LiveKit Cloud's free Build plan (no card needed) then adds one US number with 50 inbound minutes for a real call over the ordinary phone network. YouTube has genuinely free "local voice agent" builds, but none of them shows Thai, a phone line or a booking, and the receptionist tutorials all run on paid platforms. Money comes in at launch. You will need a Thai number through a licensed partner (or about $10–25 a month plus NBTC paperwork), a commercially licensed Thai voice, per-minute speech and model costs, and LiveKit beyond its free plan. Startup credits of up to $23,000 from LiveKit can cover part of that once O2 has a public product page.

## Free models can stand in for the $10 run, but cannot answer its question

The planned run is the harness in `engine/sim/model/run.ts`. It plays eight fictional spa scenarios twice for each of three Claude models (Haiku, Sonnet and Opus 5.5), with Opus also playing the caller. It **estimates about US$10 and stops itself at US$30** ([harness code](../engine/sim/model/run.ts)). It uses features only Claude has: prompt caching, an effort setting, and Anthropic's server-side refusal fallback. It does, however, talk to the model through one small interface (`ModelClient`). That means **a single adapter for the common OpenAI-style "chat with tools" format unlocks every free route below**, because Ollama, Cerebras, Gemini and Cloudflare all speak that format. The adapter has to drop the Claude-only options, including the `effort` setting the caller uses. Judging from the code's structure, one model's full run is roughly 1.6–2 million input tokens. That is an estimate; the harness logs real token counts, so the first small run will replace it.

**Running models on the Mac is the only route that is truly $0, unlimited and private.** On the Thai leaderboard SEA-HELM (18 Sep 2026), **Gemma 4 31B scores 74.30 and Qwen 3.6 27B 73.22**, the best of any model up to 35B. gpt-oss-120b, the model behind the website adviser, scores 66.88 ([SEA-HELM Thai](https://leaderboard.sea-lion.ai/detailed/TH)). On sizes, Gemma 4 12B is about 8 GB, 26B is 16–19 GB and 31B is 19–20 GB, and the family supports tool calls ([Ollama gemma4](https://ollama.com/library/gemma4)). Qwen 3.6 27B is 18–19 GB ([Ollama qwen3.6](https://ollama.com/library/qwen3.6)). Typhoon 2.5 is the Thai-made option: it was trained for tool use, and on its makers' own customer-service test it scored 50 in Thai ([Typhoon 2.5 release](https://opentyphoon.ai/blog/en/typhoon2-5-release)). It is on Ollama with tools ([Ollama SCB 10X](https://ollama.com/scb10x)). The 74.30 score belongs to the 31B model; the 12B model, the one that fits comfortably, has no Thai score of its own. No public test measures *Thai tool calling* at all, so O2's harness will be the first. One catch: Ollama's OpenAI-style endpoint supports tools but **not `tool_choice`**, and does not document strict schema checking ([Ollama compatibility](https://docs.ollama.com/api/openai-compatibility)). The harness has to check each tool call itself and count the bad ones, and that count is worth measuring anyway. Published speeds on an M4 Pro with 24 GB are about **35 tokens a second for Qwen3-30B-A3B** ([LLMCheck](https://llmcheck.net/models/qwen-3-30b-a3b-on-m4-pro/)) and **54.5 for gpt-oss-20b** ([oMLX benchmarks](https://omlx.ai/benchmarks)). That works out to roughly half an hour to two hours per model's run, by estimate. An 18–20 GB model leaves little room for macOS and Postgres on a 24 GB Mac, which makes Gemma 4 12B the safe first pick. The studio's own October tests add three warnings. gpt-oss:20b took about 5 s per Thai answer when the Mac was charging properly, and 20–60 s on a 15-watt charger. Typhoon 2.1 wrote the most natural Thai but invented web addresses and facts. All five earlier models were deleted on 11 Oct to free 48 GB, so every model now means a new 8–20 GB download, which needs your yes.

**Two free cloud tiers can carry a full run.** Cerebras's free trial gives gpt-oss-120b and Qwen 3.8 27B **1 million tokens a day per model at 5 requests a minute** ([Cerebras rate limits](https://inference-docs.cerebras.ai/support/rate-limits)). That is about one full model run every two days, by estimate. Its data-use terms were not checked. Google's Gemini API offers several current Flash and Flash-Lite models free, but **free-tier prompts are "used to improve our products"** ([Gemini pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). Human reviewers can read them, and the terms say not to send personal information ([Gemini terms](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/terms)). The daily cap shows only inside AI Studio ([Gemini rate limits](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/rate-limits)). Gemini is still the only free frontier-class model. It also makes a good *fixed caller*: the same simulated caller for every receptionist model keeps the comparison fair. Cloudflare Workers AI, which O2 already pays for, is not quite free. Every plan gets 10,000 "neurons" a day, then **$0.011 per 1,000** ([Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/)), and the website adviser draws on the same daily allowance. Working from the listed prices, one run comes to about $0.15 on Qwen3-30B or about $0.75 on gpt-oss-120b. That is cents on the bill, not zero. It is, though, the path most like production, and Cloudflare lists gpt-oss-120b with function calling and **zero data retention** ([gpt-oss-120b on Workers AI](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/models/gpt-oss-120b/)). Groq's 200,000 tokens a day ([Groq limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)) and OpenRouter's 50 free requests a day ([OpenRouter limits](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits)) would stretch one run across a week or more. GitHub Models was retired on 30 July 2026 ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/github-models/use-github-models/prototyping-with-ai-models)).

**There is no free route to Claude itself.** Anthropic's startup programme says its "current cohort is now full" ([Claude for Startups](https://www.claude.com/programs/startups)). Running the harness through a Claude subscription with `claude -p` is a policy grey zone at best. Anthropic says developers building products "should use API key authentication", and that plan limits "assume ordinary, individual usage" ([Claude Code legal](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance)). The Consumer Terms forbid automated access except through an API key or where Anthropic explicitly permits it ([Consumer Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms)). It would also measure something different: the recommended `--bare` scripting mode "doesn't use your subscription login" ([headless docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/headless)), and Claude Code's own agent loop is not the API path the harness tests. The cleanest no-cash Claude route is AWS Activate. Its credits are redeemable for Anthropic models on Amazon Bedrock (an AWS article from 2024) ([AWS Startups](https://aws.amazon.com/aws-startups/learn/aws-activate-credits-now-accepted-for-third-party-models-on-amazon-bedrock/)). The Founders tier gives self-funded companies $1,000, answers in 5–10 business days, and requires an AWS account on a paid-tier plan ([AWS Activate](https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits)). Whether the 5.5 models are on Bedrock was not checked.

| Free route | Cost to O2 | What it can test | What it cannot test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open models on the Mac (Ollama) | $0, no limit, nothing leaves the Mac | The harness, the runtime's grounding guards, Thai tool use by open models, many repeats | Claude's behaviour; server-grade speed |
| Cerebras free trial | $0, 1M tokens/day per model | gpt-oss-120b (the website's model) and Qwen 3.8 27B at full size, fast | Anything beyond the daily cap; data terms unchecked |
| Gemini API free tier | $0, cap visible only in AI Studio | A frontier-class reference and a steady caller | Any real caller's data |
| Cloudflare Workers AI | cents beyond the shared free allowance | The production-shaped path, zero data retention | A guaranteed $0 run |
| Claude via AWS credits, or the ≈$10 run | credit, or ≈$10 | The actual question: which Claude model (decision D-04b) | — |

**What the free run gives up is the question the harness was built for:** which Claude model completes the scenarios. Results from open models do not carry over to Claude. Claude's refusal fallback, the cost and speed savings from caching, the effort control and the real cost per call all stay untested. What the free run *gains* is evidence the paid run would never produce: whether an open model that O2 can run itself, or on Cloudflare for cents, handles Thai bookings well enough for production.

## Thai hearing is free and open; a Thai voice is the weak link

For speech-to-text (the receptionist's ears), SCB 10X's **Typhoon ASR Real-time is the only open Thai model that truly streams**. It has 115 million parameters, runs on an ordinary CPU, and scores 9.84% CER (character error rate) on its own card. Its licence is **CC-BY-4.0 plus the OpenTyphoon Terms** ([model card](https://huggingface.co/typhoon-ai/typhoon-asr-realtime)). Its makers' paper puts it at 9.99% CER on TV speech. Their Whisper-based models are more accurate but process speech in batches, not as a stream: **Typhoon Whisper Large-v3 6.32% and Turbo 6.85%**, against NECTEC's Pathumma-Whisper at 10.36% ([Typhoon ASR paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13044v1)). The project's own CPU example transcribes a 4.5-second clip in 1.32 s ([typhoon-asr GitHub](https://github.com/scb-10x/typhoon-asr)). Typhoon Whisper Turbo's card lists the **MIT** licence and says it suits offline transcription, not real-time streaming ([Typhoon Whisper Turbo](https://huggingface.co/typhoon-ai/typhoon-whisper-turbo)). One research note reads it as falling under the OpenTyphoon Terms instead, so check that before launch. A Whisper-turbo model transcribed a short clip in **1.02 s with mlx-whisper on an M4 MacBook Pro** ([mac-whisper-speedtest](https://github.com/anvanvan/mac-whisper-speedtest)). That is fast enough to test with. The big unknown: **no Thai model, open or paid, publishes results on 8 kHz telephone audio.** O2 has to measure it, by downsampling recordings or recording test calls. These models are tuned for Thai, so English turns need plain Whisper or a language-detection step.

For text-to-speech (the voice), the popular free voices **do not speak Thai**: there is no Thai in XTTS-v2 ([Coqui XTTS-v2](https://huggingface.co/coqui/XTTS-v2)), Kokoro ([Kokoro voices](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M/blob/main/VOICES.md)) or Piper ([Piper voices](https://github.com/rhasspy/piper/blob/master/VOICES.md)). The local voices that do speak Thai are mostly **non-commercial**: Meta's MMS-TTS Thai ([model card](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-tha)), Typhoon2-Audio's speech output ([model card](https://huggingface.co/scb10x/llama3.1-typhoon2-audio-8b-instruct)) and OpenJaiTTS ([model card](https://huggingface.co/JTS-AI/OpenJaiTTS-v1.0)). F5-TTS-THAI lists CC-BY-4.0 and copies a voice from 2–8 seconds of sample audio ([model card](https://huggingface.co/VIZINTZOR/F5-TTS-THAI)). It is a fine-tune of F5-TTS, though, and the licence of F5-TTS's base weights was not checked, so its commercial status is unclear. Mahidol's ThonburianTTS is a similar fine-tune; when its best model's speech was transcribed back, it came out at **25.72% WER (word error rate)** ([Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/items/01425a99-9731-4855-ba46-c7c77b0c9079/full)). PyThaiTTS is Apache-2.0, but nobody has published how good it sounds ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/PyThaiTTS)). These voices are fine for building and for demos with fictional callers. They are not fit for paying clients.

**The best free commercial-grade Thai voice is Azure Speech's F0 tier.** It includes 5 hours of speech-to-text and **500,000 neural text-to-speech characters a month** ([Azure pricing](https://azure.microsoft.com/pricing/details/cognitive-services/speech-services)), Thai real-time recognition, and the Thai voices Premwadee, Niwat and Achara, plus HD voices in preview ([Azure language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=stt)). Assuming the AI's side of a three-minute call is about 1,000–1,500 characters, that covers roughly 300–500 test calls a month. Google adds 60 free speech-to-text minutes a month ([Google STT pricing](https://cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/pricing/)). It also gives **4 million Standard/WaveNet and 1 million Chirp 3 HD characters a month**, but only once billing is switched on ([Google TTS pricing](https://cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/pricing?hl=pt)). Its Chirp 3 Thai recognition is still in preview ([Chirp 3](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/v2/docs/chirp_3-model)), and whether Google has Thai HD voices was not confirmed. Deepgram gives a **$200 sign-up credit** ([Deepgram pricing](https://www.deepgram.com/pricing)) and has streamed Thai with Nova-3 since 17 March 2026 ([Deepgram changelog](https://developers.deepgram.com/changelog/2026/3/17)), which makes it a good hosted baseline to compare against the local models. ElevenLabs' free plan is **non-commercial** ([bigvu](https://bigvu.tv/blog/elevenlabs-pricing-2026-plans-credits-commercial-rights-api-costs/)), and only its Eleven v3 model speaks Thai ([ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models)).

| Job | Best free option | Licence or terms | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thai hearing, live | Typhoon ASR Real-time (on the Mac) | CC-BY-4.0 + OpenTyphoon Terms | Streaming on the CPU, ~10% CER on TV speech |
| Thai hearing, most accurate | Typhoon Whisper Turbo via MLX (on the Mac) | MIT on its card; check the Typhoon terms | Batch transcription cut up by pauses |
| Thai voice, prototype | MMS-TTS Thai, F5-TTS-THAI (on the Mac) | non-commercial, or unclear | Building and demos only |
| Thai voice, client-grade | Azure Speech F0 (hosted) | free tier; data terms unread | 0.5M characters a month |
| Hosted hearing baseline | Deepgram $200, Azure 5 h/month, Google 60 min/month | credits and free tiers | Comparing against the local models |

Three wiring facts shape the build. First, **LiveKit's Azure and Google speech plugins are Python only**; Node gets Deepgram, ElevenLabs and OpenAI ([LiveKit STT plugins](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/models/stt/)). O2's engine and its LiveKit spike are Node/TypeScript ([blueprint](../BLUEPRINT-2026-11-11.md)), so Azure's free Thai voice means either a small Python voice agent calling the TypeScript engine or a custom Node plugin. Second, local models plug in through the OpenAI plugin pointed at a local server ([LiveKit OpenAI integration](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/integrations/openai/overview)). The Node plugin now defaults to OpenAI's realtime service, so set `useRealtime: false` ([LiveKit OpenAI STT](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/models/stt/plugins/openai/)). LiveKit's own $2.50 inference credit is too small to matter ([LiveKit pricing](https://livekit.com/pricing)). Third, **LiveKit's multilingual turn detector covers English plus 13 languages** ([turn detector docs](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/logic/turns/turn-detector)) and leaves out Thai ([plugin 1.3.3](https://socket.dev/pypi/package/livekit-plugins-turn-detector/overview/1.3.3)). A request to add Thai has only been acknowledged ([LiveKit community](https://community.livekit.io/t/add-language-for-turn-detection/668)). So O2 falls back to voice detection plus silence timeouts, tuned on phone recordings, because callers pause while reading out numbers ([knightli](https://knightli.com/en/2026/07/28/livekit-voice-ai-agent-telephony-sip-interruption-human-handoff-logs/)). The free speech-to-speech options are Gemini Live, where free-tier sessions have been reported rejected ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/native-audio-live-free-tier-setup-quota-rejection-below-visible-tpm-which-admission-metric-applies/186840)), and Typhoon2-Audio, which is non-commercial. Both suit experiments only. The blueprint's cascade (ears, model, voice, every step written down) is also easier to audit. Real callers' names and numbers must never go through a free tier that trains on data or lets people review it. Gemini's terms say so outright, and the data terms of Azure's, Google's and Deepgram's free tiers were not read.

## A real phone call costs O2 nothing, but a Thai number does

**Testing climbs a ladder of steps that need no phone number.** LiveKit's console mode runs the agent in a terminal and "doesn't connect to LiveKit" ([startup modes](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/server/startup-modes/)). Sources disagree on whether it works for Node agents, so O2's sure options are `dev` or `connect` mode with a browser client: the open-source agents-playground can point at the self-hosted server ([agents-playground](https://github.com/livekit/agents-playground)). LiveKit's Agent Console works with agents "running anywhere" but opens from the Cloud dashboard ([Agent Console](https://docs.livekit.io/testing/agent-console/)). The next step is a softphone. **LiveKit SIP does not accept REGISTER** (the sign-in step most phone apps expect) ([LiveKit telephony](https://docs.livekit.io/telephony/)). Instead, a softphone sends the call straight to the server, and an inbound trunk recognises it by its network address or by a username and password ([inbound trunk](https://docs.livekit.io/telephony/accepting-calls/inbound-trunk/)). So a free app (Linphone, Zoiper or Telephone.app; not yet tried here) on the Mac or an iPhone can call the self-hosted `livekit/sip` over the office Wi-Fi. That goes beyond the SIPp spike: phone-quality audio reaches the Thai speech models, and a real person handles the screened transfer.

**LiveKit Cloud's Build plan is $0 with "No credit card required."** It includes 1,000 agent minutes, 5 calls at once, **one free US local number with 50 inbound minutes** and 1,000 minutes of calls from outside phone providers ([LiveKit pricing](https://livekit.com/pricing)). Its numbers are US only ([LiveKit telephony guide](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/start/telephony/)). The worker can keep running on the Mac. A call to that number is a real call over the ordinary phone network at no cost to O2; the caller pays an international rate, which was not researched. The pricing page does not say whether claiming the number asks for a card or ID.

| Step | Cost | What it proves | What it does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text or terminal | $0 | Dialogue, Thai/English handling, tool calls into the booking engine | Any audio transport |
| Browser to self-hosted LiveKit | $0 | WebRTC, voice detection, interruptions, routing calls to the agent | Phone-quality audio (browser audio is clearer) |
| Softphone on office Wi-Fi to self-hosted `livekit/sip` | $0 (free app) | SIP call setup, 8 kHz audio into Thai speech recognition, keypad tones, screened transfer to a person | Internet delays and dropouts |
| Softphone over 4G to LiveKit Cloud | $0 | Public-internet conditions | The ordinary phone network |
| Free US number on LiveKit Cloud | $0 to O2; the caller pays international | Carrier signalling, ring timing, caller ID | Thai carriers, Thai caller-ID formats, call forwarding on Thai mobiles |

**Carrier trials give no free Thai number.** Twilio's 2026 trial gives **75 voice minutes for 30 days, no card, calls only within the sign-up country and to up to five verified numbers** ([Twilio trials](https://www.twilio.com/docs/usage/trials)). Its marketing page still promises $15 of credit ([Twilio sign-up](https://twilio.com/en-us/signup-credits)). With Thailand as a trial country, that is enough to test an outbound call to the owner's own mobile. The other trials are smaller. Telnyx gives $5 for one verified number ([Telnyx trial](https://developers.telnyx.com/docs/account-setup/using-trial-account.md)), Plivo $10 ([Plivo](https://plivo.com/pricing/)) and Vonage €2 ([Vonage](https://api.support.vonage.com/hc/en-us/articles/204014853-How-do-I-add-test-numbers-during-my-Vonage-API-trial)). SignalWire gives nothing until you top up $5 ([SignalWire](https://signalwire.com/docs/platform/trial-mode)). Thai numbers cost **$25 a month at Twilio, with inbound calls at $0.0107 a minute** ([Twilio Thailand pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/voice/pricing/th)), or **from $10 a month at Telnyx**, and both come with Thai number paperwork ([Telnyx Thailand](https://telnyx.com/phone-numbers/thailand.md)). The blueprint's legal register already rules that issuing or reselling numbers is licensed telecom business, so the business keeps its own number through a licensed partner ([blueprint](../BLUEPRINT-2026-11-11.md)). Renting a Thai number for O2's own internal testing is a separate decision for you.

**The Mac is the free host.** The agent worker connects outward to LiveKit, so it needs no inbound traffic. Only self-hosting `livekit-server` and `livekit/sip` for a real carrier needs a public IP with UDP ports open, and the free hosts fall short there. Oracle's Always Free Arm allowance is now **2 CPUs and 12 GB** ([Oracle docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)), halved in 2026 ([terminalbytes](https://terminalbytes.com/oracle-cloud-free-tier-changes-2026)), and idle machines can be reclaimed. Google's always-free VM is a US-only e2-micro, and its $300, 90-day trial needs a card ([Google Cloud free](https://docs.cloud.google.com/free/docs/free-cloud-features)). Fly's trial has no dedicated IPv4 address ([Fly trial](https://docs.fly.io/about/free-trial)), which UDP needs ([Fly UDP](https://docs.fly.io/networking/udp-and-tcp)). Railway does not support inbound UDP ([Railway](https://station.railway.com/feedback/udp-inboud-connections-9f036ffc)), and Render's free services go to sleep after 15 minutes ([Render](https://render.com/docs/free)). For Postgres, the local database is simplest. Neon's free plan needs no card but suspends after 5 minutes idle ([Neon pricing](https://neon.com/pricing)), and Supabase pauses after a week of inactivity ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)).

## YouTube teaches the free local wiring, never a Thai phone line

No video was played for this research. What follows comes from titles, third-party summaries and the linked code repositories. **The genuinely free videos are "local voice agent" builds.** The best known is "Set up a 100% Local AI Voice Agent in 10 minutes! | (LiveKit)" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=78JFdQd04Kg)), from 20 Nov 2025. It runs Whisper, Ollama with Gemma 3 4B, Kokoro and LiveKit in Docker. Its companion post claims $0 in running costs but reports **30–60 minutes of setup, about 45 GB of disk and browser-only testing** ([GrowwStacks](https://growwstacks.com/blog/local-ai-voice-agent-livekit-docker/)). A summary notes a "noticeable delay" and no tools or bookings ([readray](https://readray.ai/ms/notes/ai-agent-20260202/local-ai-voice-agent-10-min)). An updated version moves to Gemma 4 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VvGLdwSf41w); title and snippet only). Its matching repo, ShayneP/local-voice-ai, is the useful part. It is MIT-licensed and **runs natively on Apple Silicon with Metal**, using LiveKit and llama.cpp from Homebrew in about 4.7–6.5 GB of memory, with no phone support ([GitHub](https://github.com/ShayneP/local-voice-ai)). The Pipecat equivalent, kwindla/macos-local-voice-agents (MLX Whisper, Gemma3n 4B, Kokoro), reports **under 800 ms from voice in to voice out** on an M-series Mac ([GitHub](https://github.com/kwindla/macos-local-voice-agents)). That is the evidence that the Mac's GPU route, not the CPU-only Docker route, reaches replies in under a second.

**The receptionist and booking tutorials all run on paid platforms.** The Vapi and Retell builds with n8n and Google Calendar run on trial credit of about $5–10, then charge by the minute ([Layer3Labs on Vapi](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/vapi-pricing); [Layer3Labs on Retell](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/retell-ai-pricing)). The open-source AIReceptionist uses paid OpenAI Realtime at about $0.20–0.30 a minute and is AGPL-licensed. Its README shows how free routes vanish: one stopped working after OpenAI's Realtime beta ended on 3 June 2026 ([GitHub](https://github.com/kirklandsig/AIReceptionist)). These are worth reading only for the shape of the conversation and the tools. **The most valuable item is not a video at all: LiveKit's `frontdesk` example.** Its model must call `list_available_slots` before `schedule_appointment`, and a bad slot raises a `ToolError` so the model corrects itself. Ten scenarios run against a simulated caller, a fake calendar on a fixed clock and a panel of judges ([frontdesk README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/livekit/agents@main/examples/frontdesk/README.md)). That pattern matches O2's Gate A harness almost one for one. Whether the judging tool exists in the Node SDK was not confirmed.

**No Thai-language tutorial builds a voice agent or phone receptionist.** The Thai material found is a LINE chatbot on Typhoon ([LINE Developers Thailand](https://medium.com/linedevth/line-chatbot-%E0%B9%80%E0%B8%8A%E0%B8%B7%E0%B9%88%E0%B8%AD%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B1%E0%B8%9A-typhoon-llm-ai-%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%99%E0%B9%84%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%A2-%E0%B9%83%E0%B8%84%E0%B8%A3-%E0%B9%86-%E0%B8%81%E0%B9%87%E0%B8%97%E0%B8%B3%E0%B9%84%E0%B8%94%E0%B9%89-042d15660b92)) and commercial voicebots such as BOTNOI's ([BOTNOI](https://botnoigroup.com/?lang=en)). A Thai version of the free videos means swapping two parts: the ears (Whisper becomes Typhoon ASR) and the voice (Kokoro, which has no Thai, becomes a Thai voice). O2 would be ahead of anything public in Thai, and has no demo to copy for Thai quality.

| What | Rough cost | Note |

## Conclusion

Money turned out to be the easy part. The hard problems are Thai-specific and nobody has published answers: how well Thai speech recognition works on 8 kHz phone audio, when a Thai caller has finished speaking, and a Thai voice that runs locally under a commercial licence. That makes **O2's own recorded set of fictional Thai spa calls at telephone quality** the most valuable thing the free phase can produce. No vendor or researcher offers one, and it will judge every later choice of model, voice and provider. The free model run also reframes the planned spend. Ranking three Claude models was a question about quality. The free run asks a question about cost: whether an open model that O2 can host itself, or on Cloudflare for cents, handles Thai bookings reliably enough for a price-sensitive spa product. That answer is worth having before the ≈$10 Claude run, which can follow later as confirmation, ideally paid with AWS credits.

One decision quietly shapes the whole free stack, and it should be made early. Azure's free Thai voice and Google's speech plugin both need a small **Python** voice agent, and by one reading of LiveKit's docs so does its console mode. O2's engine is TypeScript. A thin Python agent that calls the existing engine keeps the booking ledger where it is and opens the best free Thai voice. Staying all-Node means writing O2's own speech plugins.
